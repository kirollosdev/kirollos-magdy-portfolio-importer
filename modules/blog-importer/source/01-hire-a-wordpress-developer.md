If you want to hire a WordPress developer, the portfolio is only the first filter. Two developers can show you equally pretty websites, and one of them will hand you a fast, secure site you can edit yourself while the other hands you a slow site that breaks the first time you update a plugin.

I have built and maintained WordPress and WooCommerce websites for clients in Canada, Germany, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Libya, and Egypt. These are the nine things I would check if I were on the other side of the table.

## 1. Look at Live Websites, Not Screenshots

A screenshot hides everything that matters. Ask for links to live projects and open them on your phone. Scroll, open the menu, submit a form, add a product to the cart. A real WordPress developer is proud to send live links because the work holds up under inspection.

Check the websites in [my portfolio](/portfolios/) the same way. That is exactly how I want them judged.

## 2. Test the Speed Yourself

Paste a portfolio link into Google PageSpeed Insights and run the mobile test. Scores vary with network and device, so do not obsess over a single number, but a developer who ships sites that load in seven seconds on mobile will ship yours the same way.

Speed is built in from the start: a lightweight theme, sized images, and only the scripts each page needs. I wrote about this in [how to build a fast WordPress website from the ground up](/how-to-build-a-fast-wordpress-website-from-the-ground-up/).

## 3. Ask Which Page Builder or Theme They Use, and Why

There is no single right answer. Elementor, Elementor Pro, Gutenberg, and custom themes all have their place. What matters is that the developer can explain the tradeoff for your project:

- **Elementor Pro** is fast to build with and easy for you to edit visually.
- **Gutenberg** is native to WordPress and very light when used well.
- **A custom theme** gives full control over markup and performance, at a higher cost.

If the answer is "I always use X" with no reason, keep looking.

## 4. Confirm They Can Write Custom Code

Most business websites eventually need something no plugin does out of the box: a custom field on a product, an API sync with your stock system, a booking rule, a form that routes to different people. A WordPress developer should be comfortable with PHP, JavaScript, custom post types, ACF, and the WordPress REST API. A site builder who only installs plugins will stack five plugins to solve one problem, and your site pays for it in speed and security.

## 5. Ask How They Handle Security and Backups

Every WordPress site needs a plan for updates, backups, and security. Ask:

- Which backup tool do you set up, and where are backups stored? (UpdraftPlus to off-site storage is a solid default.)
- Do you install a firewall like Wordfence?
- How do you protect forms from spam?

A clear answer here tells you the developer has maintained real sites, not just launched them.

## 6. Check If They Build for Your Market

If you sell in Germany, your site needs proper legal pages and often German and English content. If you serve customers in Dubai or Saudi Arabia, you probably need an Arabic RTL version. Canadian businesses often need English and French. Ask whether the developer has built multilingual and RTL websites with WPML or TranslatePress before, and ask to see one.

## 7. Make Sure You Can Edit the Site Yourself

You should not need a developer to change a price, a phone number, or a photo. A good WordPress developer builds editable templates, uses dynamic content where it makes sense, and gives you a short guide or recording showing how to update your pages.

## 8. Agree on Ownership and Handover

Before you pay, confirm in writing that:

- The domain and hosting are in your name.
- You receive admin access and all premium plugin licenses or a clear list of them.
- Any custom plugin or theme code is yours.

This protects you if you ever change developers.

## 9. Judge the Communication, Not Just the Code

Remote work only works with clear communication. Notice how fast and how clearly the developer replies before the project starts. Do they ask about your business goals, or only about colors? Do they explain what they will deliver and when? That is how the whole project will feel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to build a WordPress website?

A simple business website usually takes two to four weeks. A WooCommerce store or a site with booking, multilingual content, or custom integrations can take six to ten weeks depending on content readiness and revisions.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?

A freelancer is usually faster and more affordable for small and medium projects, and you talk directly to the person building your site. An agency makes sense when you need a large team for design, content, and marketing at the same time.

### What should I prepare before I contact a developer?

Your goals, a list of pages, examples of websites you like, your logo and brand colors, and any content you already have. The clearer the brief, the more accurate the quote.
