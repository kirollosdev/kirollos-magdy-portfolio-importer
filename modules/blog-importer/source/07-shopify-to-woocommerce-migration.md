A Shopify to WooCommerce migration is not just copying products. Done carelessly, it breaks your Google rankings, loses customer accounts, and confuses returning buyers. Done carefully, customers barely notice anything except a store that finally does what you need.

This is the checklist I follow.

## Why Stores Move From Shopify to WooCommerce

Common reasons I hear:

- Monthly app costs keep growing.
- Transaction fees when using a payment gateway other than Shopify Payments.
- Features that need deep customization, like B2B pricing or custom checkout rules.
- Wanting full ownership of the store and data.
- Better content and SEO control on WordPress.

If you are still deciding, read [WooCommerce vs Shopify](/woocommerce-vs-shopify/) first.

## Step 1: Audit What You Have

Before moving anything, list:

- Number of products, variants, and collections.
- Apps you use and what each one does.
- Customer accounts and order history you need to keep.
- Your current URLs, especially top traffic pages from Google Search Console.
- Payment and shipping settings.

Every Shopify app needs a WooCommerce equivalent or custom code. This is where hidden work shows up.

## Step 2: Set Up WooCommerce on a Staging Site

Never build on your live domain. I set up WordPress and WooCommerce on a staging site with good hosting, a lightweight theme, and the essential plugins only.

## Step 3: Migrate the Data

- **Products**: titles, descriptions, prices, SKUs, stock, images, variants, and categories. Shopify's CSV export can be mapped to WooCommerce's importer, or a migration tool can be used for larger stores.
- **Customers**: names, emails, and addresses. Passwords cannot be moved, so customers will reset their password on first login. Plan an email to tell them.
- **Orders**: historical orders for records and customer accounts.
- **Reviews**: exported and imported so social proof is not lost.

After import, I check a sample of products manually. Variants and images are where problems usually appear.

## Step 4: Rebuild the Design

This is a chance to improve, not just copy. I rebuild the store with Elementor Pro or a custom theme, keeping what converts well and fixing what does not. Product pages, cart, and checkout get the most attention.

## Step 5: Map URLs and Set 301 Redirects

This step protects your rankings. Shopify uses paths like /products/ and /collections/, and WooCommerce uses different ones by default. Every old URL needs a 301 redirect to its new equivalent. I export all old URLs, map them one by one, and test them before launch.

## Step 6: Recreate SEO Settings

- Page titles and meta descriptions, set in Yoast SEO.
- Image alt text.
- Product schema.
- XML sitemap submitted to Google Search Console.

## Step 7: Configure Payments, Shipping, and Taxes

Set up gateways like Stripe, PayPal, or regional providers, shipping zones and rates, and tax rules. Then test real orders with every payment method.

## Step 8: Test Everything, Then Launch

Test on mobile and desktop: search, filters, add to cart, coupons, checkout, emails, and account pages. Then switch the domain, check redirects again, and monitor Search Console and sales for the first weeks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will I lose my Google rankings?

Some movement is normal right after a migration. With complete 301 redirects and preserved content, rankings usually settle. Missing redirects are what cause lasting losses.

### How long does a migration take?

A small store with a few hundred products can take two to four weeks including design. Larger catalogs or complex apps take longer.

### Can my customers keep their accounts?

Their accounts and order history can be migrated. They will need to set a new password.
