A WordPress developer for Germany has to build more than a good-looking site. German customers expect trust, clarity, and legal correctness, and German online shops face stricter rules than many other markets. I have built and optimized WooCommerce stores for the German market, including bilingual German and English content, ecommerce tracking, and speed work.

Here is what a website for Germany needs.

Impressum and Legal Pages

German websites need an Impressum (legal notice) with the operator's details. Since May 2024, the legal basis is Section 5 of the Digitale-Dienste-Gesetz (DDG), which replaced the old Telemediengesetz (TMG), so older Impressum pages that still reference the TMG should be updated. Online shops also need terms and conditions, a cancellation policy, and a privacy policy.

I set up these pages and their links correctly across the site. The legal wording itself should come from a lawyer or a trusted legal text service.

GDPR and Cookie Consent

Under GDPR and German rules, non-essential cookies and tracking need consent before they load. That means:

  • A consent banner with equal accept and reject options.
  • Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager tags, and ad pixels that respect consent.
  • Self-hosted fonts instead of loading them from external servers where possible.
  • A privacy policy that matches the tools actually used.

I configure consent mode and tracking so analytics still works for visitors who accept, without firing for those who do not.

German and English Content

Many German businesses sell to both German and international customers. With WPML or TranslatePress, the site gets proper German and English versions, with translated product data, checkout, emails, and SEO. German copy should be written or reviewed by a native speaker, because German customers notice unnatural translations immediately.

Payment Methods Germans Trust

German shoppers have strong payment preferences. A German store usually offers:

  • PayPal, which is very widely used in Germany.
  • Pay later and invoice options such as Klarna.
  • Cards: Visa and Mastercard.
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay.
  • Bank-based options.

I integrate these through gateways like Stripe, PayPal, and Klarna. More in WooCommerce payment gateway integration.

German Shop Requirements

German stores need clear price display with VAT included, shipping costs visible before checkout, and a checkout button that clearly states the order is binding with payment. These details affect both compliance and trust.
