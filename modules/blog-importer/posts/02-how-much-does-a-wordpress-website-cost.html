How much does a WordPress website cost? The honest answer is anywhere from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands, and the range is that wide because "a website" can mean five static pages or a multilingual store synced with a warehouse system.

This guide breaks the cost into the parts you actually pay for, so you can compare quotes and budget with confidence. The numbers below are typical freelance ranges in US dollars. Agencies usually charge more, and every project is quoted on its real scope.

What You Pay For: The Four Cost Buckets

Every WordPress project has four cost areas:

  1. Design and development: the work to plan, design, and build the site.
  2. Hosting and domain: where the site lives and its address.
  3. Premium themes and plugins: licenses such as Elementor Pro, WP Rocket, or WPML.
  4. Maintenance: updates, backups, security, and small changes after launch.

Most quotes only cover the first bucket. Ask about the other three before you sign.

Typical Build Costs by Website Type

Business or Portfolio Website

A five to ten page site with a custom design, contact forms, basic SEO setup, and a responsive layout. Expect roughly $800 to $2,500 with a freelance WordPress developer, depending on design complexity and how much content you provide.

Custom-Designed Website From Figma

If a designer has already made your pages in Figma, the developer converts them into an editable WordPress site. The cost depends on the number of unique templates, animations, and dynamic sections, typically $1,500 to $5,000.

WooCommerce Online Store

A store needs product pages, cart, checkout, payment gateways, shipping rules, tax settings, and transactional emails. A standard WooCommerce build commonly lands between $2,500 and $8,000. Custom features such as stock sync with an internal system, subscriptions, or B2B pricing push it higher.

Booking, Travel, and Hotel Websites

Tour operators and hotels need availability calendars, seasonal pricing, and online payments, often through plugins like WP Travel Engine or VikBooking. These builds usually start around $2,500 and grow with the number of trips, rooms, and integrations.

Multilingual and RTL Websites

Adding a second language with WPML, especially Arabic with a right-to-left layout, typically adds 20 to 40 percent to the build cost, because every template has to be tested and adjusted in both directions.

Yearly Running Costs

These are the costs that continue after launch:

  • Domain: usually $10 to $20 per year for a .com.
  • Hosting: $100 to $400 per year for quality shared or managed hosting for a small business. Busy stores need more.
  • Premium plugins: often $50 to $300 per year combined, depending on what the site uses.
  • Maintenance plan: from about $50 per month for updates and backups to a few hundred for sites that need regular changes. I cover this in detail in WordPress website maintenance cost.

What Makes a WordPress Website More Expensive

  • Custom functionality: API integrations, custom plugins, custom post types with advanced filtering.
  • Number of unique page templates: ten different layouts cost more than ten pages built from one layout.
  • Animations and interactive effects: they look great but take time to build and to keep fast.
  • Content creation: writing copy, sourcing photos, or entering hundreds of products.
  • Multiple languages and right-to-left support.
  • Tight deadlines.

Where Cheap Websites Cost You Later

A very low quote usually means a heavy multipurpose theme, a pile of plugins, and no plan for speed or security. That site will often be slow on mobile, hard to edit, and expensive to fix. Rebuilding a site a year later almost always costs more than building it properly once.

How to Get an Accurate Quote

Send a developer:

  • Your goals: leads, sales, bookings, or information.
  • A list of pages or a sitemap.
  • Two or three websites you like, and why.
  • Your content status: ready, partial, or not started.
  • Any must-have features: languages, payments, booking, integrations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is WordPress cheaper than Shopify?

WordPress itself is free, and you pay for hosting, plugins, and development. Shopify has a monthly subscription and app fees. For stores, the long-term cost depends on your sales volume and needs. I compare them in WooCommerce vs Shopify.

Can I build a WordPress website myself?

Yes, for a simple site. Once you need custom design, speed optimization, payments, or integrations, a developer saves you time and prevents expensive mistakes.

Do I pay the full amount upfront?

Most freelancers work with milestones, for example a deposit to start, a payment after design approval, and the balance at launch.
